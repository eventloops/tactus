## 3. Readings taken on packet ambiguities

Each is a place where the packet, the errata and the shipped vocabulary together do not determine

### R1 — tier and pin of a one-off binding (errata E2)

E2 amends `decisions.task_registry.binding_override`: the validation binds *"the complete resulting
rung binding: agent, model and effort from the override payload, and tier and pinned from the
frozen option selected by option_index"*. The shipped frozen option is a bare agent name
(`SpawnAdmission::HumanBinding { options: Vec<String> }`, filled from `allowed_agents`), so tier
and pin cannot come from the option literally.

**Reading.** `tier` is the repair ladder's frozen `floor` and `pinned` is `true`. Both are
derivable from frozen registry state alone, which replay stability requires: the fold re-derives
them on every replay of every log. `repair_ladder` sets `floor = max(root floor, mid)` on exactly
the empty-intersection path that raises a `HumanBinding`, so the floor is the tier the options were
offered *at*; "the frozen option" is read as "the frozen option set", whose tier is that floor.
`pinned: true` because a human naming one agent for one task is the definition of a pin, and it is
the safer value: a build that forgets to mark it is refused rather than admitted.

**Alternatives rejected.** Leaving tier and pin unbound (the shipped half-rule, which E2 exists to
close). Deriving the tier from the model catalogue, which is a compile-time constant and would make
the replay of an old log depend on the binary. Widening the frozen option to a rung — a Class C
change to `SpawnAdmission` (§6).

The abandoned attempt reached the same reading.

### R2 — where a one-off binding's `model` and `effort` come from

`BindingOverride` carries `{key, question, option_index, agent, model, effort}`; the answer source
yields an option *text*, an agent name. Nothing in the packet says who chooses the model.

**Reading.** The model is the named agent's catalogued model at the lowest tier at or above the
ladder's floor (`crate::catalog::CATALOG`, first entry at that tier in catalogue order), and the
effort is the entry's frozen effort policy at the floor. The derivation runs **once, live, at
ingest**, and what replay reads is the recorded payload; the fold never re-derives the model, so a
compile-time catalogue is safe here and would not be inside the fold. An agent with no catalogued
model at or above the floor refuses the activation before any append, naming the agent and the
floor.

**Alternatives rejected.** `run_started.chains` (frozen run identity, but the empty intersection
means the root's chain has nothing at or above the floor, and another task's chain is not the
right authority for this task). A new seam on `AttemptPlans` for the assembler to choose — a
reasonable future shape when PR12 wires production config, recorded here as a follow-up, not
built: the production `FrozenPlans` has no config today either.

### R3 — the ceiling applies to a repair dispatch

`select` returned `Step::RepairDispatch` *before* the ceiling check, because the checkpoint refused
the step anyway. `decisions.sequential_substrate.loop` states the branch as *"else if a ready task
exists: ceiling check, provisional dispatch reservation, dispatch"*, and a repair is a ready task.
**Reading:** a repair dispatch checks the ceiling exactly as an ordinary dispatch does, and a breach
appends `budget_exceeded` naming the repair's key. Nothing exempts repairs, and `repairs.limits`
bounds them by count, not cost.

### R4 — which settlement folds a lineage (`decisions.repairs.terminal_failure`)

*"a lineage member Failed folds every AwaitingRepair member of that lineage to
Failed{lineage_failed} and releases the lineage lease"*. **Reading:** the fold folds the lineage
when a task **that has a `lineage` in the registry** settles `attempt_finished{Closed{Failed}}` —
a repair, never an ordinary task. A lineage root is an ordinary task and is `AwaitingRepair` with no
open generation for as long as its lineage lives, so it cannot be the settling task; testing
membership rather than `lineage_root(key)` keeps an ordinary task's terminal failure exactly as PR7
left it. `Failed{lineage_failed}` is a derived-state annotation, not a wire field: `TaskState::Failed`
carries no payload, and adding one would be Class C.

### R5 — classifying the materialization (measured, not assumed)

`attempt_started.materialization_observed` is `Clean | Conflict | Empty | Retained`, and nothing
says how a `git cherry-pick --no-commit` maps onto the first three. Measured on this box (git 2.43.0,
`x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`) on purpose-built repositories, 2026-09-08:

| case | exit | `ls-files --unmerged` | `diff --cached --quiet` | git-dir residue left |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the change applies | 0 | empty | 1 (a staged change) | `AUTO_MERGE`, `MERGE_MSG`, `ORIG_HEAD` |
| the change is already present | 0 | empty | 0 (nothing staged) | `AUTO_MERGE`, `COMMIT_EDITMSG`, `MERGE_MSG`, `ORIG_HEAD` |
| the change conflicts | 1 | three stage entries | — | `AUTO_MERGE`, `COMMIT_EDITMSG`, `MERGE_MSG`, `ORIG_HEAD` |
| the commit is not an object | 128 | — | — | — |

Two facts from the table bind the code. First, **`CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` is not left behind by
`--no-commit` in any of the three cases** — `git rev-parse --verify CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` fails in all
three worktrees — which contradicts the pre-existing doc comment on `repair_materialize` ("`--no-commit`
deliberately leaves `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` behind"). `MERGE_MSG` is left by the bare command, and it is
one of the names `administrative_residue_at` reads; the funnel therefore removes `MERGE_MSG` and
`AUTO_MERGE` once the pick has ended, in all three outcomes (PR9-F2, §9), so a *completed*
materialization is quiescent at its base and `Worktree.Verify` reuses it (`Reuse::Verified`), while
a pick killed before that removal leaves `MERGE_MSG` or its held `.lock` and fails the verify. (The
first version of this paragraph said a completed materialization fails the verify "exactly as an
interrupted one does" — true of the bare command in the table, false of the funnel since PR9-F2;
PR #249's second-round record review caught the contradiction, §13 finding 4.) Second, exit 0 with
an unchanged index is the already-present case, not an error.

**Reading.** Exit 0 with a staged change is `Clean`; exit 0 with none is `Empty`; a non-zero exit
**with unmerged index entries** is `Conflict` and is not an error — a conflicted cherry-pick is the
normal shape of repairing a `Conflict` rejection, and leaving the conflict in the worktree is what
gives the agent something to resolve. Any other non-zero exit stays the Git error it is today.
`Retained` is recorded by a same-generation retry, which re-materializes nothing.

### R6 — when a repair generation's worktree is re-materialized

`T-REPAIR-DISPATCH.resume_action`: Worktree.Verify at the recorded base; on failure remove with
force and recreate; re-run the cherry-pick deterministically; the observed kind is recorded when
`attempt_started` is appended. `recovery_order` (g): "recreate OpenNoAttempt worktrees at their
bases (through Worktree.Verify or forced recreate)". Neither says whether step (g) or the loop's
continuation performs the cherry-pick.

**Reading.** Recovery step (g) recreates the worktree **at its base only** — verify, or forced
recreate — for ordinary and repair generations alike. The materialization is reproduced by the
loop's continuation of the generation, immediately before the `attempt_started` that records what
it observed. Two reasons. The observation recorded must be the one made by the process that
appends it; an interrupted process's observation is never carried. And one materialization per
resume: a step (g) that materialized would hand the loop a worktree the continuation materializes
again — two cherry-picks per resume for one observation. What the code does, in sequence, when a
coordinator dies after a completed materialization and before `attempt_started`: (g) verifies the
existing generation and gets `Reuse::Verified`, because the funnel cleared `MERGE_MSG` and
`AUTO_MERGE` when the pick ended (R5 as corrected); the already-materialized index is retained as
it is; the continuation restores the base's tree and picks once. So every resume of a repair
generation performs exactly one cherry-pick, on a worktree that verified at its base or was just
recreated there, and the three prefixes a kill can leave (before the pick, inside it, after it)
converge on the same tree — because the funnel restores the base's tree before it picks (§12,
finding 3), not because a pick onto an index that already holds it is a no-op: measured, it is not.
`a_continuation_after_a_completed_pick_hands_the_worker_the_tree_one_pick_produces` asserts
`Reuse::Verified` on both of its resumes.

**Alternative rejected.** Materializing in step (g) as well (the abandoned attempt's shape).
Correct and deterministic, but it performs the effect twice per resume. (The first version of this
paragraph said the alternative would report a healthy worktree as `Recreated { Residue(MergeMsg) }`;
that premise died with PR9-F2's clearing of the state files — §13, finding 4.)

### R7 — which candidate a repair is materialized from, and where that is read

`repairs.dispatch`: "task_dispatched(repair) records base and source candidate; ... git cherry-pick
--no-commit <candidate> from the protected candidates ref". `merge_rejected`: "the rejected
candidate's candidates ref remains as the protected repair source". DESIGN §26.4 says of a
semantic rejection that the engine "materializes the clean proposal against that current head".

**Reading.** The source is always the **rejected candidate** — the `candidate` the `merge_rejected`
that registered the repair names — because that is the object R11 protects: the proposal's pin is
pruned at the rejection terminal and the staging worktree reclaimed, so the proposal is Git's, not a
protected source. Cherry-picking the candidate onto the current head reproduces the proposal for a
code rejection and the conflict for a textual one, which is what §26.4 describes in both arms.

For a **fresh** dispatch the source is read from the `merge_rejected` event whose `repair.key` is
the task (the log is the durable pairing). For a **continuation** of an open generation — live, or
after a resume — the source is the `task_dispatched.source_candidate` of that generation, read from
the log. Both reads are Class A: `integrate::authorized_head` and `IntegrationRequest::from_log`
already read the log for exactly this kind of fact, and it avoids retaining a second copy in the
frozen fold (the abandoned attempt's Class B row 1, not taken here).

### R8 — the materialization kind on a same-generation retry

ST-15: "a repair generation retry records `LineageHeld` and does not re-materialize". **Reading:**
the retry records `Materialization::Retained` and runs no `Object.RepairMaterialize`; the fold keeps
checking only presence parity (a lineage member records a kind, an ordinary task records none) and
does not gain a refusal on the kind itself. The kind is the engine's observation, like `pool`, and
the packet's refusal list for this slice names no refusal for it.

### R9 — what "unresolved index entries fail capture before gates" can mean

`repairs.dispatch` and DESIGN §26.4: "the worker may edit but never commit; the engine refuses a
result with unresolved index entries and then runs the ordinary gates and reviews." Under
*agents edit files; the engine owns git*, an agent cannot resolve an index entry: only the engine's
own staging (`git add -A` at capture) does, and staging resolves every unmerged path
unconditionally — an untouched conflicted file is staged **with its markers inside**. Read
literally after staging the rule is vacuous; read literally before staging it fails every conflict
repair, because no agent may run `git add`.

**Reading (revised twice: §12 finding 2, then §13 finding 1).** The rule is read where the design
states it, in the index: before the capture stages anything, the engine lists the index's unmerged
paths, and every one of them is conflicted — whatever the working-tree file looks like, so a
`conflict-marker-size=8` or `-merge` conflict is read like any other. What resolves one is a
declaration, not a git command: the worker resolves the file with its file tools and records the
path in the resolution manifest, `.upstroke-resolved` at the root of its worktree, as
`resolved <path>` (the working-tree content, or its absence, is the resolution) or
`deleted <path>`; at capture the engine stages what the manifest declares
(`git add -- :(literal)<path>` or `git rm --quiet -- :(literal)<path>`, inside `Object.CandidateStage`,
before the `add -A` that now excludes the manifest) and refuses the capture if any unmerged path is
undeclared, the manifest does not parse, or a path is declared both ways — staging nothing,
returning the base's tree, and failing the attempt as the worker's failure (`AgentError`, naming
the entries) before any gate or review runs. §4's "the engine owns git" holds literally: the worker
runs no git command at all, and `design/20`'s "gates are the only commands they may run" is
untouched. A path that does not decode is reported as one unresolved entry naming the reason. The
manifest is read from the durable worktree at capture time like the diff; it is not an event
field, so no wire vocabulary changes (Class C: none).

**The readings it replaces, and why.** The first reading inferred from "the engine owns git" that a
worker cannot resolve an index entry, and so read the working-tree files for `<<<<<<< ` and
`>>>>>>> ` markers instead, calling a file without them resolved and a missing file resolved by
deletion. PR #249's conformance review materialized a conflict under `conflict-marker-size=8` and
another under `-merge`: the scan read both as resolved, the capture staged everything, and
unresolved work reached the gates. The second reading (§12 finding 2) kept the index as the record
and told the worker to stage its resolution with `git add`/`git rm`, "exactly Git's own hint" —
and no supported worker can: PR #249's regression review assembled the production Claude Code
permissions (`Read, Glob, Grep, Edit, Write, NotebookEdit, Bash(<gate>)` under `dontAsk`) and
Copilot's (`write`, `shell(<gate>)`), and the owner's design-staging session measured that Codex's
`workspace-write` sandbox keeps `.git` read-only and that its one switch admitting `git add` admits
`git commit` too. A binary keep-ours resolution is, in the file bytes, identical to abandonment, so
no content read can replace the worker's signal either; the declaration is the signal, and the
engine — which owns staging — acts on it. Option 4 of
`~/tactus-artifacts/DESIGN-STAGING-DECISION.md`, ruled by the owner; §13 finding 1.

### R10 — the answer-ingestion branch, and why it needs a non-blocking read

`loop`: "after recovery: ingest answers (never after budget_exceeded or a halting settlement in
this epoch) -> if an eligible integration exists ...". PR8 assigned this branch to PR9 by name
(`LoopBranch::IngestAnswers => Disposition::NotThisSlice { slice: "PR9", .. }`) and ingested only
verification-park answers, at the hard block. DESIGN §4 (6): "Questions never stop the runnable
frontier ... the run hard-blocks only when nothing remains runnable."

**Reading.** Every `step()` begins by polling the answer source for each open question, in id
order, while the run is not ending; the first answer or decline is ingested as `question_answered`
and the step returns `Progress::Answered`. The hard block keeps its blocking read. The poll must be
**non-blocking**: `TerminalAnswers::resolve` prompts and reads stdin, and `EventLogAnswers::resolve`
sleeps up to its budget — both are the hard block's "attached-terminal prompt or wait_on_block",
and neither may run while other work is runnable. So `AnswerSource` gains `poll`, defaulting to
`Unanswered`; `EventLogAnswers` answers it from the answer file without waiting. That is the change
this reading asks of `interaction.rs`; the first repair round changed the file again — an option is
a decline by what it says (`DECLINE_OPTIONS`, `is_decline_option`, `answer_for_option`), with its
tests — §12 finding 1. (This sentence once called the poll "the whole change to `interaction.rs`",
which it stopped being at `a23ce25c`; §13, the P3.)
